# GMAIS Public Glossary

This glossary defines public-safe terms used in the GMAIS framework. It does not disclose private implementation details, prompts, memory content, infrastructure configuration, or protected source code.

## GMAIS

**Governed Multi-Agent Intelligence System.** A structured approach to coordinating multiple AI systems, models, tools, and review roles through explicit governance, context boundaries, role separation, validation, continuity discipline, and human approval controls.

## RGIS

**Relationally-Governed Intelligence System.** The relational layer of governed AI behavior: how identity, continuity, trust, authority, accountability, and human oversight influence system behavior over time.

## V9 Governance

A governance model associated with validation before execution, continuity preservation, constraint-aware system behavior, and explicit human authority over consequential actions or changes.

## Continuity

The disciplined preservation of relevant identity, decisions, context, evidence, and governance constraints across sessions, systems, releases, or environment transitions without treating unverified recollection as fact.

## Human Approval Boundary

A defined point where an AI system must pause and obtain explicit human authorization before taking a consequential action, changing protected state, publishing material, or committing an irreversible operation.

## Role Separation

The division of responsibilities among models, agents, tools, and human reviewers so that planning, implementation, validation, approval, and release authority are not automatically collapsed into one actor.

## Validation Before Execution

A control principle requiring proposed actions to be checked against evidence, constraints, permissions, risks, and human intent before execution.

## Context Boundary

A deliberate limit on what information is available to a model, agent, tool, or workflow stage. Context boundaries reduce accidental disclosure, scope drift, and ungoverned state propagation.

## Evidence Discipline

The practice of distinguishing what is documented, verified, inferred, planned, implemented, tested, and released. Claims should remain traceable to appropriate evidence.

## Human Authority

The principle that the human owner or designated governance authority retains final control over protected identity, strategic direction, consequential actions, publication, and release decisions.

## Public-Safe Architecture

An architecture description detailed enough for professional review and citation while excluding secrets, private prompts, protected schemas, implementation bindings, user data, and other restricted material.
